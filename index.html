Premium Parking: Customer Retention & Loyalty Strategy

The Problem

Premium Parking is a technology-driven parking operator in New Orleans running a unified SQL data warehouse across transaction, occupancy, pricing, and location data. Most customers parked once and never came back, and nobody had quantified how much of that was actually preventable churn versus one-time visitors who were never coming back anyway.

What I Did

I extracted and cleaned the transaction-level dataset directly from SQL and did the bulk of the analytical coding myself (AI-assisted via Copilot for speed, fully reviewed and validated). Rather than impose arbitrary frequency buckets, I let the data define its own groups. Four natural customer segments emerged from visit-frequency breakpoints already present in the behavior:

TierVisit frequencyShare of customers
Explorers1 visitLargest single segment
Bronze2–5 visitsSecond-largest segment
Silver6–20 visitsSmall, high-value segment
Gold20+ visitsSmallest, highest-loyalty segment

On top of that segmentation, I designed a 3-tier loyalty program with deliberately asymmetric incentives. Discounts where they'd actually change behavior (Explorers and Bronze), and status perks rather than discounts for Silver and Gold, since those customers weren't price-sensitive and discounting them would have just given away margin for free. To test whether the strategy was worth building, I ran a Monte Carlo simulation (1,000 simulations per scenario) modeling revenue impact under different tier-upgrade adoption rates, then built a phased rollout plan sequenced around the company's highest-revenue locations and seasonal demand patterns.

Result

The simulation showed a clear, positive return even under conservative adoption assumptions, net of all projected discount and points costs. It was strong enough that we presented it as a full go-to-market plan, including a three-phase geographic rollout and a launch date timed ahead of the company's seasonal peak. Exact financial figures are covered by an internal NDA and aren't shared publicly; I'm glad to walk through the actual numbers and methodology in an interview.

Note: this was a capstone practicum project for the Freeman School, not a paid deliverable. The strategy was presented to leadership but has not yet been implemented.

Second Line Capital: AI Micro-Lending for NOLA Small Businesses

The Problem

New Orleans has roughly 16,000 small businesses, many of them cash-heavy, seasonal, and tourism-driven, including restaurants and food trucks that traditional banks routinely reject because FICO scoring can't read cash flow, collateral, or community trust. Black-owned businesses in particular face dramatically higher denial rates, and most of the businesses turned down aren't actually failing; they're just invisible to the underwriting model.

My Role

As team lead, I helped shape the core concept alongside my team and then built the public-facing website for the venture. The idea: an AI underwriting engine, the "Second Line Score," that reads POS transaction history, review sentiment, social engagement, and event bookings instead of a credit bureau file, issuing decisions in 24 hours instead of weeks. Repayment is revenue-based (a percentage of daily POS receipts, capped and self-correcting), so a slow week doesn't trigger default the way a fixed loan payment would.

The Pitch

We modeled a four-year path from a 75-loan pilot to operating profitability, with the long-term business positioned not as lending itself but as licensing the underlying credit-scoring model to CDFIs and community banks in other underbanked cities once it's proven in New Orleans. We didn't place in the competition, but the team carried the idea through a full business model, a financial model, and a live site, the complete loop from problem to pitch.
